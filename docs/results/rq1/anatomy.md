# RQ1 descriptive anatomy

AEI data are conversations matched to O*NET tasks. Nothing here says anything about job risk, displacement or reassurance about the labour market.

## α split

RQ1's α = 0.05 is split (D22): H2 is tested on its own at α = 0.0125, and {H1, H3, H4} by Holm at family-wise α = 0.0375, so the RQ1 family-wise error is at most 0.05. This document tests nothing; the split is stated because every RQ1 results document states it.

## Scope and reading

- Composition is computed over published cells only: what AEI leaves out is not imputed, and each table's usage shares are renormalised to 100 over the cells it covers.
- The occupation basis is AEI's published SOC rows (D9). The task-allocated basis is not used here.
- The reference release is r6 (claude_ai); its two months are combined by D13: a value missing in one month is averaged over the month that has it, never treated as 0.
- Cross-release concentration mixes suppression regimes (PAP §5), so differences between releases are not read as changes in the underlying conversations.
- These tables are descriptive. No hypothesis test is attached to any of them, and they report no p-value.
- Patterns are percentages of classified conversations. Automation = directive + feedback loop; augmentation = task iteration + validation + learning.

## Usage concentration across tasks, as published

One row per release and source over the published matched tasks, usage renormalised to 100. `topK_share` is the usage share of the largest ⌈K% · n⌉ tasks; `n_tasks_50pct` is the number of largest tasks that together hold half the usage. The columns ending `_pos` repeat the statistics over the tasks with positive usage only; `n_zero` counts the tasks published as 0.

In r6, usage is published to two decimals, and earlier releases publish finer values. Both drive the gap between the as-published rows of r6 and r1-r5: the zero-usage tasks it keeps in its task count and Gini (zero inclusion), and its coarser rounding (which turns small shares into those zeros).
